Greening Youth Foundation × National Park Service

Turning interns into the content.

The HBCU Internship program (HBCUI) places students from Historically Black Colleges and Universities at national park sites across the country. I ran its Instagram, @hbcui_gyf, for a summer. The best stories were happening at dozens of parks I'd never visit, so I built a system that let the interns bring them to me.

01 — Challenge

The story was happening everywhere but here.

HBCUI interns spent the summer at national parks, monuments and historic sites all over the country, from Olympic National Park to the Birmingham Civil Rights National Monument. I worked remotely and had no photos, no footage and no access to the sites.

The account had three jobs:

  • Show the programMake each intern's work visible to partners like the National Park Service and the National Park Foundation.
  • Build communityGive interns at separate sites a reason to follow, engage with and cheer on each other.
  • RecruitShow HBCU students what a summer in the parks could look like, and drive applications.

I inherited an intern spotlight series and redesigned it starting my first day, May 26, 2023. Then I built everything else around one idea: the interns are the content.

02 — Spotlights

A template that made 40+ posts feel personal.

Every intern got their own post. The design and the caption followed one system, so I could publish them quickly while each one still felt written for that person.

Design
I kept the program's existing look and leaned into travel and conservation symbolism: a globe, field-notebook paper, a framed photo like a travel keepsake, and the National Park Service and Greening Youth marks in the corners. Name, school, role and park sit in a fixed order so the series reads as one set.
Caption formula
A short line about the intern's work, written from their major, then "I'm [name], proudly representing [HBCU] as [role] at [park]." Following the brand's earlier captions kept the voice consistent while each opener stayed specific to the person.
Search and discovery
Every spotlight carried keywords people actually search for: the park's name, the college, the job title, and motivational vocabulary. Hashtags did the same work. Each post could be found by the intern's school, the park, and the program.
Why it worked
Each spotlight gave an intern, their family, their school and their park a reason to like, comment and share. That built-in audience is why engagement grew faster than reach.

03 — Challenges

Campaigns that collected the content for me.

I ran challenges for two reasons: to get interns and followers engaging, and to get photos and video from sites I couldn't reach. Every entry was a post I didn't have to source.

I also ran Pass the Post, where each intern shared their internship in four photos, in uniform, then nominated the next person.

04 — The system

Project coordination behind every post.

Dozens of interns, dozens of sites, one account. Nothing got posted without a process for getting it to me.

  1. BriefSent interns documents explaining exactly what to submit and by when: photos, bios, videos, challenge entries.
  2. CollectOrganized submissions in shared Drive folders and followed up by email, messages, calls and the team's internal channels.
  3. DesignBuilt each graphic and caption in the program's look, using the spotlight template and caption formula.
  4. PublishPosted across four content pillars: spotlights, challenges, park site features and conference coverage.
  5. DocumentCreated documentation of the work for management, so the program had a record of every intern and every post.

05 — Results

Engagement grew twice as fast as reach.

Instagram Insights for May 31 to July 12, 2023, compared with the six weeks before, showed the account reaching 527% more people and engaging 1,145% more.

Engagement outpacing reach is the signature of community content. The people who saw these posts had a personal reason to respond: it was their friend, their classmate, their park, or themselves.

The summer, documented.

For the end-of-term conference I coordinated and designed a 44-page yearbook documenting 30+ participant projects, delivered on deadline to 100+ stakeholders. It carried the same scrapbook look as the feed, so the whole summer read as one brand. Here are pages for the program's leaders and site supervisors.

07 — Reflection

What I'd keep, change, and test next.

What worked

  • Making the people in the program the subject of the content.
  • One template and caption formula, so volume never cost quality.
  • Challenges that doubled as a way to collect photos and video.

What I'd do differently

  • Track results per challenge: entries, reach and new followers.
  • Save Insights snapshots monthly, not just once.
  • Turn more intern video into Reels.

What I'd test next

  • Intern takeovers on Stories.
  • A recurring weekly series for park facts.
  • Collab posts with each park's own account.
